What comes after the pilot: rag-formation-continue

Your 30-day plan gets you a defensible pilot. Here's where to go next once real users, real feedback, and real production traffic show up.

Today's tangible win

A map of the five things rag-formation-continue covers, and the one to read first based on your own pilot's weakest spot.

1 · The skill

rag-formation-continue is a separate, free tutorial series — not part of this course's numbered syllabus — written from real production experience operating a RAG system like the one you just built. It picks up exactly where Lesson 16 leaves off: a defensible pilot and a system that survives contact with real users are not the same thing.

  • Log it, and let users rate it: log every interaction and add one thumbs-up/thumbs-down button, before touching retrieval — the least glamorous change, and the only ground truth you'll have.
  • Context windows are not retrieval: why a bigger context window still doesn't solve cost and latency, access control, freshness, or traceability.
  • Four retrieval channels, not one: chunk-vector, summary-vector, full-text, and graph search each cover a different blind spot.
  • From triage to fix: read your own failures, categorize them with an LLM, and hand a scoped, checkable bug to a coding agent.
  • Build your own, from zero: a prompt for the whole four-channel pipeline, if you're starting over or explaining the baseline to someone else.

Mission connection

This course's mission stops at a defensible pilot: a prototype whose limitations are on the table, with a disciplined path toward production. rag-formation-continue applies that same discipline — read your own evidence before acting — to what happens after the pilot ships: real users, real feedback, real failures worth triaging.
